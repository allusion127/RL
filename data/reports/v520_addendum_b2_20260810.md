**The §6.2 power disclosure does not rescue it.** That clause was written to
catch a REFUTES obtained on a degenerate sample; it required the realized spread
to be reported first, and it fires only below 0.030. The measured spread is

for a top-5 hedge to pay, and it did not: the cheap-policy claim failed on a
surface that was fully able to express it. That is the strongest form the
batch-1 result could have taken, and it is why it is worth replicating rather
than repairing.

Two things batch 1 did **not** settle, and this batch is aimed at both:

1. **Replication.** `P1 − P5` from a single 20-candidate draw is one number with
   no interval. The ORACLE sat at predicted-rank 18 — i.e. the model's ranking

   0.65 the K2 simulation assumed). A second independent draw is the cheapest
   check that 0.0071 is a property of the mechanism and not of one pool.
2. **Low-F_r production.** Batch 1 landed **7 cores under F_r 1.50**, best
   **1.4784** — below the cell's stored incumbent 1.4636? No: *above* it, but
   inside its immediate neighbourhood, and **all 20 are novel**. The cell's
   stored F_r p10 is 1.52498, so 7 of 20 under 1.50 is a materially better yield
   than the stored distribution. **This value does not depend on the K2 verdict**

## 3. What is NEW in batch 2

### 3.1 A new generation seed — 4784

**`random_seed = 4784`**, with the registered top-up ladder **4784, 4785, 4786, …**
consumed in ascending order and only as needed.

Chosen before the draw, and named rather than arbitrary: the deck's own
convention is that the seed names the F_r it is chasing (`fpcamp_minfr_199.inp`
uses 4636 for the stored incumbent 1.4636). **4784 names batch 1's ORACLE core,
F_r 1.4784.** It is disjoint from batch 1's registered ladder (4636–4643), of
which only 4636 was consumed, so no batch-2 draw can be a re-draw of a pool
batch 1 already looked at.

Everything else about generation is byte-identical to batch 1. **The seed is the
only difference in the generator's inputs** other than the novelty oracle of
§3.2 — which is what makes this an independent draw of the same mechanism rather
than a different experiment.

### 3.2 Novelty must exclude batch 1 BY HAND — the merge has not landed

The pre-registration §2.4 defines novelty against the canonical store. That is
insufficient here, and the reason is timing:

> **Batch 1's 20 verified patterns are being merged into `records.parquet` by
> another agent right now, and at batch-2 generation time the merge had NOT
> landed.** Verified directly rather than assumed: of batch 1's 20 candidates,
> **0/20 record_ids and 0/20 packed patterns** are present in the store snapshot
> the batch-2 generator read.

**The store therefore cannot exclude them, and two batches sharing a pattern
would not be independent draws** — it would also silently re-verify a core whose
F_r is already known, which §2.4 forbids outright. So batch 2's novelty oracle is
the union of:

1. the **current canonical store** (re-read at generation time; row count, byte
   count and sha256 recorded in §7 and inside `candidates.json` under
   `store.sha256`), **and**
2. an explicit hand-exclusion of the 20 batch-1 patterns, read from
   `runs/v520/candidates.json` (`v520_gen.py --exclude-json`), applied by packed
   pattern **and** by campaign `record_id`.

The generator records whether that hand-exclusion was **load-bearing** — i.e.
whether the store would have caught those patterns anyway. Given the 0/20 above
it is expected to be load-bearing, and §7 states the measured value. This is
recorded because "we excluded them" and "the exclusion mattered" are different
claims and only the second one justifies the extra machinery.

If the merge lands before generation runs, the store test catches batch 1 on its
own and the hand-exclusion becomes redundant — **but it is passed regardless**,
because a redundant exclusion costs nothing and a missing one silently destroys
the independence of the replication.

### 3.4 THE DRAWS ARE INDEPENDENT IN SEED BUT **NOT** IN PARENTS

**This outranks every other caveat in this document and it changes what the word
"replication" is allowed to mean here.**

Between §3.2's verification (batch-1 merge not landed) and the batch-2 pool
being drawn minutes later, **the merge landed**. The store went 70,984 → 71,015
rows and batch 1's 20 patterns went from 0/20 to **20/20 present**. The §3.2
hand-exclusion therefore turned out **not** to be load-bearing — the store caught
them on its own. That part is harmless: novelty holds either way, and the
belt-and-braces exclusion cost nothing.

**What is not harmless is what the merge did to the elite parents.** `build_pool`
seeds its elite-mutation arm from `_store_elites()`, which ranks this cell's
constraint-feasible converged rows by `−F_r·1e6 + cyclen` and takes
`elite_top_k = 32`. Batch 1's cores are among the lowest-F_r rows the cell has
ever held, so they entered that set immediately. Measured, not assumed:

| | value |
|---|---:|
| cell converged rows, before the merge / after | 742 / **762** |
| constraint-feasible rows | 183 |
| **top-32 elite parents that are BATCH-1 cores** | **10 / 32** |
| their F_r | 1.4784, 1.4922, 1.4928, 1.4935, 1.4950, 1.4993, 1.4999, 1.5001, 1.5025, 1.5056 |
| batch 1's ORACLE (1.4784) rank among all elite parents | **#2**, behind only the stored incumbent 1.4636 |

**So batch 2 is a SEQUENTIAL replication, not an independent one.** Its pool is
conditioned on batch 1's labels through roughly a third of its parent set,
including the single best core batch 1 found. Registered now, before any batch-2
label exists:

* **This is not a mistake to apologise for — it is what a real campaign does.**
  Wave *N+1* is always conditioned on wave *N*'s labels; that is the operating
  regime the K2 policy question is actually about. But it is **not** the
  "independent draw" the follow-up job asked for, and calling it one would be
  false.
* **Direction of the effect on the LEVEL is signed and expected:** batch 2's
  parents are better than batch 1's were, so batch 2's realized F_r *level*
  (P1, P5, ORACLE alike) should be lower. **Direction of the effect on the
  DIFFERENCE `P1 − P5` is NOT signed a priori** and must not be asserted in
  either direction after the fact.
* **The one asymmetry that IS foreseeable, and is registered as such:** mutating
  a tighter, better parent set can narrow the realized F_r spread, and a
  narrower spread mechanically compresses `P1 − P5` (pre-registration §6.2:
  `P1 − P5 ≤ realized spread`). **A batch-2 REFUTES obtained on a realized
  spread materially below batch 1's 0.1524 is therefore partly attributable to
  the narrowed surface, and the verdict must say so.** Batch 2's realized spread
  is reported FIRST, as §6 requires, and compared to 0.1524 explicitly.
* **§5's no-pooling commitment is unchanged and now matters more**, because two
  sequentially-linked batches are *less* independent than two draws would be:
  the mean-of-two remains the only registered combined statistic, and no
  interval may be computed from n = 2 correlated batches.
* **If the coordinator wants a strictly independent replication**, it requires a
  third draw generated against a store snapshot with batch-1 (and batch-2) rows
  excluded from `_store_elites()`. That is a machinery change, not a deck
  change, and it is **not** done here — doing it silently would break "the
  champion's own proposal machinery", which is the sentence the whole design
  rests on. It is named here as the available option, and left to the
  coordinator.

The pre-merge store snapshot (`4039bc96…`) has been overwritten on disk and no
copy is known to exist, so a strictly-independent batch 2 could no longer be
generated from this session even if it were wanted. That is recorded rather than
hidden — the same class of provenance loss `ab2_addendum_R3_20260801.md` §3.4
recorded for the round-1 store.

## 5. THE NO-POOLING PRE-COMMITMENT — fixed now, before batch 2 exists

This is the part of this addendum that exists to constrain the author, and it is
registered in advance precisely because the outcome of batch 1 is already known
and the outcome of batch 2 is not.

### 5.1 Both batches are reported together, always

**Every downstream citation of this experiment must report BOTH `P1 − P5`
values side by side, with their batch labels, their seeds and their box.**
Batch 1's `+0.0071` may never appear without batch 2's number once batch 2 has
run, and batch 2's number may never appear without batch 1's. There is no
circumstance in which one batch is the result and the other is an appendix.

### 5.2 Neither batch may be dropped after its labels exist

Batch 1 is in the record. Batch 2 enters the record the moment its chains
complete. **Neither may be excluded for any reason discovered after its labels
exist** — not for an unexpected spread, not for an unfavourable Spearman, not
for the ORACLE landing at an awkward rank, not for the box. The **only**
admissible exclusion is a §5.2 validity-gate failure, which is a pre-registered
rule evaluated before the policy numbers are read, and whose consequence is VOID
+ re-run of the failed chains, not deletion.

### 5.3 The ONE combined statistic, named in advance

To make cherry-picking impossible rather than merely discouraged, exactly one
combined number is registered, and no other may be presented as "the combined
result":

> **Combined estimator = the unweighted mean of the two per-batch `P1 − P5`
> values**, evaluated against the SAME thresholds of §4.

Explicitly **forbidden**, because each would manufacture a result neither
batch's model call ever produced:

* **pooling the 40 labels into one 40-candidate ranking** and reading a "top-1"
  and "top-5" off it — the model never ranked those 40 against each other, and
  the resulting P1/P5 would be an artifact of concatenation;
* re-ranking across batches by any key, predicted or realized;
* reporting "the better of the two batches", "the batch with the cleaner
  Spearman", or any max/min over batches;
* weighting the batches by spread, by convergence, by box, or by anything else;
* computing an interval from n = 2 and presenting it as evidence.

### 5.4 The registered readings, all four of them

| batch 1 | batch 2 | registered reading |
|---|---|---|
| REFUTES | **REFUTES** | **K2 refuted, with replication.** The strongest conclusion available from this design: the verification-policy shortcut is not the cheap win, the model is the bottleneck, and memo §3's mechanism ordering (ADF first, per the STEP 0 verdict) stands unchallenged by this line of attack. |
| REFUTES | **GRAY ZONE** | K2 not supported; the mean-of-two is reported and will itself sit below 0.030 unless batch 2 is near the top of the gray band. Reported as "one refutation, one inconclusive" — **not** as a partial vindication. |
| REFUTES | **SUPPORTS** | **UNREPLICATED.** Neither batch may be cited alone. The mean-of-two is reported together with the plain statement that a two-batch disagreement at n = 20 each is what it looks like when the quantity is dominated by draw-to-draw noise. **Registered consequence: a third batch is required before any policy decision, and no model-upgrade priority may be re-argued on the strength of either single batch.** |
| (VOID) | any | If either batch is VOID on its own gates, it is repaired per §5.2 of the pre-registration or it is reported as VOID. A VOID batch is never quietly replaced by the other one. |

### 5.5 The production value is unconditional

Batch 2's 20 labels — every one of them, converged or not, low-F_r or not —
enter the record and the store regardless of which row of §5.4 fires. **A
disappointing K2 number may not be used to suppress, delay or de-prioritise the
data.** The low-F_r production purpose (§1, item 2) was declared before the
draw and is independent of the policy verdict; that is why both purposes are
named in this document rather than one being discovered afterwards.
