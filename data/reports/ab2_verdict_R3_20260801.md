## 4. Why this is REJECT, and what a reader must not do with that

R3 improved the median |err| on **four of six** axes on the decision surface
(`map_cov` −6.0%, `node_peak` −4.25%, `cbc_max` −2.1%, `f_r` −1.15%), established
the programme's first gain, and left the rails cleaner than any prior arm. It
degraded `cyclen` by 6.1% and the pre-registered clause 2 says zero
established-worse axes. **So it is REJECT.**

The tempting move is to say the trade is obviously worth it — especially since
pre-registration §1's own table marks `cyclen` at 0.74 EFPD as the one axis that
**passes** its acceptance bar, so a small `cyclen` regression spends margin that
exists, to buy movement on axes that are 20–70× off their bars.

**That argument may well be right, and it must not be applied here.** It was
constructed after seeing which axis regressed. Applying it now would convert
three rounds of pre-registration into a post-hoc analysis, which is the exact
failure the apparatus exists to prevent (`flat_ab` module docstring, guarantee
3: "a near-tie goes to the user"). The registered rule is the rule.

**The correct response is procedural, and it is available:** round 4 may
register — *before* seeing any result — a bar that prices `cyclen` explicitly:
e.g. "zero established-worse among {`node_peak`, `map_cov`, `f_q`, `cbc_max`},
plus `cyclen` non-inferiority at ε = 0.25 EFPD", justified by §1's margin table.
Under such a rule R3's cyclen harm bound (upper bound 0.290 EFPD) would be
tested rather than assumed fatal. **This is a recommendation for how to write
round 4's rule, not a re-scoring of round 3.** R3 stays REJECT.

## 7. What this obliges

Addendum §5.8's falsification is met, and it was written narrowly on purpose:

> **What is falsified is the TRANSFER hypothesis** — "labels added in f121
> improve the f101–f141 holdout cells" — **not the mechanism "more labels in the
> deficient cells".** The mechanism was never exercised on the decision surface:
> zero enriched rows are in it (addendum §2.1).

Strictly, even the transfer hypothesis survives in weakened form — `map_cov` did
transfer, reproducibly. What failed is that transfer came bundled with a
`cyclen` cost the registered rule does not permit.

| §1 mechanism | status after three rounds |
|---|---|
| loss / label engineering | **falsified** (round 1) |
| ensemble / variance reduction | **falsified** (E10) |
| more labels in deficient cells | **not falsified — untested on the surface**; first-ever gain observed, transfer partial |
| verify-many-then-select | **untested** |

**Forbidden** by the registered consequences: `--ensemble 20`, larger
`--traj-weight`, larger `--map-peak-topk-weight`, and **another undirected
store-growth round scored on this same f121-free surface**.

**Round 4 must settle, in this order:**

1. **Fix the surface/mechanism mismatch.** Hold out a slice of the enriched f121
   cells so densification is measured where it happened. Three rounds have now
   been limited by pre-registration §5.1's surface: A2 was inert on it (100%
   `master_native`), the E10/A3 off-surface signal lives outside it, and R3's
   enrichment never touched it. **This is the highest-value change available and
   it is not a model change.**
2. **Explain the five-for-five `cyclen` regression.** Every arm, every
   mechanism, degrades `cyclen` against A0 while `M5_cell_rho_cyclen` stays
   bounded — a level effect, not a ranking collapse. If A0 sits at a `cyclen`
   optimum that any perturbation leaves, that is a property of the recipe worth
   knowing before the next arm is designed.
3. **Decide, in advance, whether `cyclen` margin is spendable.** §4's proposed
   explicit `cyclen` non-inferiority band would have made round 3 decidable on
   its merits. Register it before round 4 runs, or don't use it at all.
