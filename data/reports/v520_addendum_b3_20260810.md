## 4. Novelty — three sources, all applied

A batch-3 candidate is admitted only if its packed pattern **and** its campaign
`record_id` are absent from **all three** of:

1. the canonical store snapshot read at generation time (sha256 in §8.1);
2. **batch 1's 20 patterns** (`runs/v520/candidates.json`);
3. **batch 2's 20 patterns** (`runs/v520_b2/candidates.json`).

Both hand-exclusions are passed to `v520_gen.py --exclude-json` **regardless of
merge status**, and the generator records for each whether it was load-bearing
(i.e. whether the store would have caught those patterns anyway). Redundancy
costs nothing; a missing exclusion destroys the disjointness of the three
batches.

## 5. THE THREE-BATCH DECISION RULE — fixed now, before batch 3 exists

The b2 §5 no-pooling commitment is carried over in full and extended to three:
**all three `P1 − P5` values are reported together, always, with their batch
labels, seeds and boxes; none may be dropped after its labels exist; the only
admissible exclusion is a pre-registered validity-gate failure.** Pooling the 60
labels into a single 60-candidate ranking remains **forbidden** — the model never
ranked those 60 against each other.

### 5.1 The rule

**Primary estimator (unchanged from b2 §5.3, extended to three): the unweighted
mean of the three per-batch `P1 − P5` values, against the SAME thresholds.**

**Concordance guard (new, and the reason the rule is not the mean alone):**

```
SUPPORTS K2   iff  mean(P1-P5) >= 0.030  AND  >= 2 of 3 batches have P1-P5 >= 0.010
REFUTES  K2   iff  mean(P1-P5) <  0.010  AND  >= 2 of 3 batches have P1-P5 <  0.010
UNRESOLVED at n=3   otherwise
```

**Why a guard at all.** The mean is the right *estimand* — over many batches you
bank the average gain, not the median one — but `P1 − P5` is non-negative and
right-skewed (it is `P1 − min(top-5)`, zero whenever rank 1 happens to be the
best of the five, large whenever rank 1 happens to be bad), so its three-sample
mean is dominated by its largest draw. The observed 11× batch-to-batch ratio is
not a hypothetical: **without a guard, batch 2 alone would decide the
programme.** The guard requires the mean and a majority of batches to agree
before either verdict is issued, and routes every disagreement to UNRESOLVED.

**The median-of-three is reported as a registered robustness readout and is NOT
a criterion.** It is named here so it cannot be introduced later as if it had
been: it is reported, it is not decisive, and it may not be substituted for the
mean in any citation.

### 5.2 Pre-emptive power disclosure — what batch 3 can and cannot decide

Registered before the draw, from arithmetic on b1 + b2 alone:

* Sum of the two known values = **0.0855**.
* **REFUTES is arithmetically unreachable.** `mean < 0.010` would need
  `b3 < −0.0555`, and `P1 − P5 ≥ 0` by construction (`P5 = min` over a set
  containing `P1`). No batch-3 label can produce an overall REFUTES.
* `mean ≥ 0.030` needs only `b3 ≥ 0.0045`; the **binding** constraint is the
  concordance guard, which — since b1 is already below 0.010 — requires **both**
  b2 and b3 to clear 0.010. b2 does.
* **Therefore batch 3 decides exactly between `SUPPORTS` (b3 ≥ 0.010) and
  `UNRESOLVED` (b3 < 0.010), and nothing else.**

This is stated in advance so the outcome cannot afterwards be presented as a
richer or more symmetric test than it is.

### 5.3 Stopping rule — batch 3 is the last

**No batch 4 is run under this registration.** If batch 3 lands UNRESOLVED, the
registered conclusion is that the K2 policy effect *exists but cannot be sized at
n = 20 × 3*, and the consequence is a **design change** — variance reduction, a
different estimand, or a different cell — **not another batch.** Adding batches
until the verdict settles is the same error as moving the threshold, arrived at
more slowly.

## 6. THE NULL THAT THE ORIGINAL THRESHOLD DOES NOT CLEAR

This section registers a mandatory co-reported quantity. **It does not change the
§5 verdict rule** — inventing a new criterion after seeing two batches is exactly
the post-hoc move this programme rejects — but it constrains, in advance, what a
`SUPPORTS` verdict may be claimed to *mean*.

### 6.1 The random-ranking null

If the model's ranking within a 20-candidate pool were **pure noise**, then P1 is
a uniform draw from the batch's own 20 realized F_r values and P5 is the minimum
of 5 draws without replacement from the same 20. Both expectations are exact by
enumeration over order statistics — **computed from the batch's own labels, with
zero extra MASTER**:

```
E_null[P1 - P5]  =  mean(F_r)  -  E[min of 5 of the 20]
```

Measured on the two batches already in hand:

| | batch 1 | batch 2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| observed `P1 − P5` | 0.0071 | 0.0784 |
| **random-ranking null `E[P1 − P5]`** | **0.0404** | **0.0242** |
| observed / null | **0.18×** | **3.23×** |
| **excess over null** | **−0.0333** | **+0.0542** |
| Spearman(pred, real) | +0.3100 | −0.3143 |

Mean null over the two = **0.0323**. Mean excess = **+0.0105**.

### 6.2 What that means, registered before batch 3

> **The `SUPPORTS ≥ 0.030` bar sits BELOW the measured random-ranking null of
> 0.0323. A model with ZERO ranking skill would, in expectation, "SUPPORT K2"
> under the registered rule.**

The bar is therefore not, on its own, evidence that verification-many is
valuable; it is close to what falling back on chance already yields. And the
measured Spearmans (+0.31, −0.31, mean ≈ 0 against the simulation's assumed
0.65) say the champion's within-pool F_r ranking **is** close to chance on these
elite pools.

**Registered interpretive constraint, binding on the verdict text:**

* A `SUPPORTS` verdict **must** be reported together with the three per-batch
  nulls and the three-batch mean excess-over-null.
* If the three-batch **mean excess over null is ≤ 0**, the verdict text **may
  not** claim that the verification policy beats model upgrades. The registered
  sentence in that case is: *"top-5 verification recovers F_r because the
  champion cannot rank within an elite pool — which is an argument for repairing
  the ranker, not for preferring the policy to it."*
* If the mean excess over null is **> 0 and the sign is consistent across at
  least 2 of 3 batches**, then the policy gain exceeds what a broken ranker
  explains, and K2's cost argument stands on its own terms.
* The thresholds of §5 are **not** modified by this section under any outcome.

This inverts the use K2 makes of a positive result, and it is registered now,
before batch 3, precisely because that inversion would otherwise look like a
post-hoc rescue of the refuted position.

### 8.3 The §3 conditioning, measured

Reconstructed against the exact draw-time snapshot (the current store minus the
20 batch-2 rows — an exact reconstruction, since that is the only delta):

| | value |
|---|---:|
| draw-time cell converged rows / constraint-feasible | 762 / 183 |
| **top-32 elite parents that are batch-1 cores** | **10 / 32** |
| top-32 elite parents that are batch-2 cores | **0 / 32** (b2 not yet merged) |
| **batch-3 candidates whose parent is a batch-1 core** | **15 / 20** |
| parent is another store row | 2 / 20 |
| no parent (`guided` / diversity origin) | 3 / 20 |

**Three quarters of batch 3 is one mutation away from a batch-1 core** — up from
10/20 in batch 2. Batch 2's cores, by the accident of merge timing, are **not**
in batch 3's ancestry at all. So batch 3 is conditioned on batch 1 and not on
batch 2, which is worth stating precisely: the three batches are **not** a chain
(b1 → b2 → b3) but a **fan** (b1 → b2, b1 → b3). Neither structure is
independent sampling, and §5's concordance guard and §5.3's stopping rule are
registered on that understanding.
