R3's registered falsification clause named this arm explicitly as the next move
when its own null landed. **Admissible.**

The claim under test is not a model claim. It is an *operating-policy* claim:

> With the champion's existing, unchanged F_r ranking skill, spending the batch's
> MASTER calls on the model's **top 5** candidates instead of its **top 1**
> recovers more realized F_r than any model upgrade currently on the table —
> at **zero** model change, zero retraining, zero new features, zero migration.

The numbers K2 quotes, restated here so the bar cannot drift:

| quantity | value | source |
|---|---:|---|
| simulated top-1-of-20 realized F_r regret at ρ = 0.65 | 0.0636 | memo §4 K2 |
| simulated top-5-of-20 realized F_r regret at ρ = 0.65 | 0.0092 | memo §4 K2 |
| **simulated gain of the policy change** | **0.0544** | memo §4 K2 |
| same simulation, ranking skill lifted 0.65 → 0.80, top-1 | 0.0335 | memo §4 K2 |
| realized fuel-lever ΔF_r (8 arms, 1 improved) | −0.0070 | memo §4 K2 / `flat_assembly_fr_verdict_20260809.md` |

Two of those rows are the whole point. **Improving the model's ranking skill by
0.15 Spearman is worth less (0.0636 → 0.0335) than changing the number of
verified candidates from 1 to 5 (0.0636 → 0.0092).** If that survives contact
with MASTER, every model-upgrade proposal in memo §3 must clear it on
information-per-MASTER-call, not on R².

**Everything above is a SIMULATION.** It was computed by resampling the stored
within-cell F_r distribution under an assumed rank correlation. This
pre-registration exists because a simulated 0.0544 is not a measurement, and the
program's own standard (five arms rejected on pre-registered rules) does not
admit one.

### 2.3 Which 20 — the selection rule

Applied in this order, all four steps registered before the pool existed:

1. **Admissibility** — keep pool entries with `in_region == True` and finite
   `rank` (the campaign's own trust-region hard gate; out-of-region rows carry
   `rank = −inf` and a real wave can never adopt them).
2. **Novelty** (§2.4) — drop any candidate already in the store.
3. **Distinctness** — dedupe by packed pattern (`Pattern.canonical()`), keeping
   the higher-`rank` representative.
4. **Cut** — take the **top 20 by `scored.rank` descending**, i.e. the
   campaign's own exploit preference order, the identical key `_proposals_only`
   prints and the identical key an exploit slot would adopt.

**Registered top-up rule, in case fewer than 20 survive steps 1–3 on the first
pool.** Re-draw with `random_seed` 4637, then 4638, … in ascending order, one
draw at a time, appending only candidates that pass steps 1–3 and are distinct
from everything already held, and stop at the first draw that reaches 20. The
per-candidate seed provenance is recorded in `candidates.json`. This is
registered now so that "how many pools did you look at" can never become a
post-hoc degree of freedom.

### 2.4 Novelty — required, and defined by packed-pattern equality

**Re-measuring a stored core is not verification.** A candidate is admitted only
if BOTH hold against the frozen store snapshot of §3.1:

* its packed pattern string `Pattern.canonical()` appears **nowhere** in
  `records.parquet` (**any** library, **any** pair, **any** feed — deliberately
  the widest test), and
* its campaign identity
  `compute_record_id(canonical, "ga80", "E1_E2", "ga80_produce")` — the
  `CAMPAIGN_DECK_KNOBS` path of `construct.candidate_record_id`, which is the
  same id the campaign's own store write uses (`campaign.py:1687`,
  `verify.PRODUCE_DECK_KNOBS`) — is **not** a `record_id` in the store.

Both tests are kept because they are not equivalent: dataset A rows were
identified with `deck_knobs = "mocha_default"` and dataset B rows with
`"ga_native"`, so the *same* 69 cards can sit in the store under a record_id
that the campaign hash would never reproduce. Pattern equality catches those;
record_id equality catches an exact campaign-path duplicate. A candidate failing
**either** test is dropped.

### 2.5 Ranking — the model's predicted F_r, ascending

The 20 held candidates are ordered by **predicted mean F_r ascending**
(`ScoredPool.mean[:, 0]`, the ensemble mean — not the UCB the acquisition
scalar uses, not the exploit rank). Ties broken by ascending `record_id`.
`predicted_rank = 1` is the model's single best guess at the lowest-F_r core.

This ordering is **frozen to disk in `runs/v520/candidates.json` at generation
time, before any MASTER call**, together with each candidate's packed pattern,
SHF text, predicted `(f_r, cbc_max, f_q, cyclen, ao_abs)`, `p_feas`, `acq`,
`rank`, `origin` and seed provenance. The file's sha256 is printed by the
generator and must be quoted in the verdict. **A ranking that can move after the
labels arrive is not a ranking.**

All 20 are evaluated through `WaveVerifier.evaluate_wave`, configured exactly as
`5_RL/fr_transfer.py` configures it (that path produced the store-comparable
`fr_transfer_E3E4` and `fr_arms_*` measurements):

| `library_dims` | read from `lib/MAS_XSL` — `(COMP + 3, COMP + REFL)` = **(83, 85)** |

**Realized F_r** is `FOM.F_r` (EDIT3 FRP cycle maximum) — the same scalar the

**Flatness scalars** come from `lpopt.data.flatness.slot_values` →

sweep).

### 2.7 The three policies — counterfactual, from the SAME 20 labels

There is **no separate baseline run**. All three policies are read off one set
of 20 labels, which is what makes the experiment cost 20 chains instead of 40:

| policy | definition |
|---|---|
| **P1** | realized F_r of the candidate at `predicted_rank = 1` |
| **P5** | `min` realized F_r over `predicted_rank ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}` |
| **ORACLE** | `min` realized F_r over all 20 |

By construction `ORACLE ≤ P5 ≤ P1`. The K2 quantity is **`P1 − P5`**: the
realized F_r a batch recovers, at fixed model, by verifying five candidates
instead of one.

## 5. The decision rule — fixed before any candidate exists

### 5.1 Primary criteria — verbatim, and these numbers do not move

```
SUPPORTS K2  :  P1 − P5 >= 0.030
REFUTES  K2  :  P1 − P5 <  0.010
GRAY ZONE    :  otherwise (0.010 <= P1 − P5 < 0.030) — report only
```

The 0.030 bar is K2's own ("≳0.03 F_r"), not one chosen after the fact. The
0.010 floor is the memo's stated consequence of failure and is registered with
its reading attached:

> **A REFUTES verdict means the verification policy is NOT the cheap win, and
> the model IS the bottleneck.** In that case the model-upgrade proposals of
> memo §3 — v7 channel 55 (ADF), the burnup-placement arm §3(0), the trajectory
> fusion head §3(4) — recover their priority, and the "0.0544 for free" argument
> against them is withdrawn in writing.

A GRAY ZONE result promotes nothing and refutes nothing. It is reported with its
interval-free status stated plainly; §6.2's spread readout is what a reader
should weigh alongside it.

### 5.2 Validity gates — ALL must hold, or the verdict is VOID

Checked before the criteria of §5.1 are evaluated, never after:

| # | gate | why |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | **all 20 chains converged** (`status == "converged"`, `fom` present) | a missing label makes P1/P5/ORACLE mean different things on different subsets |
| V2 | **all 20 at `fallback_level == 0`**, and every harvested row reads `restart_provenance == "native:MAS_RST.APRQ_11_0635.19"` | a substituted restart changes the physics between candidates |
| V3 | **the 20 patterns are pairwise distinct** (packed-pattern equality) | a repeated pattern silently duplicates one draw in the ranking |
| V4 | **none of the 20 already has a converged row in the store** (§2.4, both tests) | re-measuring a stored core is not verification, and a stored core is one the search has already seen |
| V5 | the frozen `candidates.json` sha256 quoted in the verdict matches the file the runner consumed | the ranking must predate the labels |

**Registered remedy for a V1 failure:** re-run only the failed chains from the
**unchanged** `candidates.json` (the runner is resumable and skips candidates
already present in `v520_results.jsonl`), then re-check V1. Re-generating,
re-ranking or substituting candidates for the failures is **forbidden** — that
would let the label outcome select the sample.

**V2/V3/V4 failures are not repairable after the fact** and route to VOID.

### 5.3 What each verdict changes

| verdict | consequence, registered now |
|---|---|
| **SUPPORTS** | verify-5-per-batch becomes the default campaign policy for this cell, and every model-upgrade proposal must be argued against a measured, free 0.03+ F_r baseline — on realized F_r per MASTER call, not on held-out R². |
| **REFUTES** | the policy argument in memo §4 K2 is withdrawn; the model is the bottleneck; memo §3's mechanism ordering (ADF first, per the STEP 0 verdict) stands unchallenged by this line of attack. |
| **GRAY ZONE** | neither. The arm is reported and the decision defers to the §6.2 spread readout and to cost. |
| **VOID** | nothing is concluded from the labels; §5.2 names the only admissible repair. |

## 6. Registered SECONDARY readouts — none of them can change the verdict

### 6.1 Predicted-vs-realized Spearman over the 20

`spearmanr(predicted_f_r, realized_f_r)` across all 20. **The K2 simulation
assumed ρ = 0.65** (the champion's measured within-cell F_r rank correlation).
This is the direct check of that input, on this exact population.

Registered in advance, because it is the one number that can explain either
outcome without changing the verdict:

* ρ materially **below** 0.65 → a SUPPORTS is *understated* (worse ranking makes
  top-1 worse and the top-5 hedge more valuable) and a REFUTES needs no rescue.
* ρ materially **above** 0.65 → the simulation was pessimistic about the model,
  and a REFUTES is partly "the model is already good enough at this", which is a
  different sentence from "the policy does not work". **It is reported as such
  and it still does not move the §5.1 thresholds.**

### 6.2 Realized spread — the pre-emptive power disclosure

`P1 − P5 ≤ P1 − ORACLE ≤ max(realized F_r) − min(realized F_r)`.

**If the realized F_r spread over the 20 is below 0.030, SUPPORTS is
unreachable by construction, and a REFUTES verdict then carries far less
information than it appears to** — it would say "these 20 elite candidates were
all alike", not "verifying more does not pay". This is the same class of defect
R3 disclosed in `ab2_addendum_R3_20260801.md` §2.1 (mechanism not exercised on
the surface), and it is registered before the labels for the same reason.

The threat is concrete: the K2 simulation drew from the cell's **stored**
within-cell F_r distribution (§3.1: min 1.4636, p10 1.52498, p50 1.8129 — a
spread of order 0.35), whereas these 20 are the exploit-ranked head of an elite
pool and will be far more concentrated. **The realized spread is therefore
reported first in the verdict, before P1/P5/ORACLE**, and any REFUTES is
qualified by it.

### 6.3 Also recorded, verdict-inert

* `P1 − ORACLE` and `P5 − ORACLE` (how much of the achievable gain each policy
  captures), and the predicted rank of the ORACLE candidate.
* Per candidate: full `FOM` (`F_r`, `F_q`, `CBC_max`, `cyclen`, `|AO|`),
  `node_peak`, `map_cov`, `n_cycles`, wall time, `restart_provenance`, and the
  saved harvested plane `map_rank<NN>_<record_id[:12]>.npy`.
* Count passing the program feasibility screen (`F_r ≤ 1.55`, `CBC ≤ 1550`,
  `F_q ≤ 2.41`, `620 ≤ cyclen ≤ 645`) — the `SCREEN` of `fr_transfer.py`.
* Whether any of the 20 beats the cell incumbent F_r 1.4636.
* Sensitivity: `P1 − Pk` for `k = 2, 3, 4, 5, 10, 20`. **Reported as a curve,
  never as a menu of thresholds** — only `k = 5` is registered, and only the
  `k = 5` number may be cited in a verdict.

* The *predicted* spread does **not** bound `P1 − P5`; the **realized** spread
  does, and the two are different quantities. The champion's own F_r error in
  this cell is ~0.021 (median |pred − MASTER|, `fpcamp_minfr_199.inp` header) —
  **twice the predicted spread** — so the realized spread can be, and on the
  cell's own statistics probably is, several times wider than 0.0100. The
  feasible subset of this cell (n = 168, F_r ≤ 1.55) has sd 0.0159 and range
  ≈ 0.086.
* **This is not a defect of the design; it is the mechanism under test.** K2's
  claim is precisely that when predicted differences are smaller than prediction
  error, the ranking cannot be trusted at the top and the hedge across five
  candidates pays. A predicted spread narrower than the model's own error is the
  regime where that claim lives or dies.
* §6.2 stands unchanged: the **realized** spread is reported first, and a
  REFUTES verdict accompanied by a realized spread below 0.030 must be reported
  as "these 20 elites were alike", never as "verifying more does not pay".
